# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Working keyboardists who play live — any gigging player, not just the Jewish-music circuit Corda started in. Their context is the hardest one to design for: on stage, mid-set, one hand on the keys, the other on the phone, in a dark room under time pressure with an audience watching. The job is to pull up the right song, read the chart at a glance, get it into the singer's key, and keep moving without breaking the performance. Corda is a public, multi-user product; every player brings their own library, so accounts, per-user data, and a shared/clone-able repertoire are on the horizon (auth is currently open).

## Product Purpose

Corda is a performance chord book for live keyboardists. It holds the song library, renders chord-over-lyric charts, transposes on the fly, and runs a full-screen Gig Mode built to be read from a music stand during a set. Prep is meant to approach zero: AI fills in chords and keys so a song is stage-ready without manual charting, and a key finder recommends the right key for the room. Success is a keyboardist getting through a live set faster and more confidently with Corda than with paper, a PDF, or a generic chord app — never fighting the tool while the music is playing.

## Positioning

Corda is a gig instrument, not a chord archive: it generates charts on the fly, transposes and finds the right key in the moment, and drops into a stage-ready Gig Mode — built for any working musician, tuned by real live use rather than any single genre.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, fast, pro-grade. Corda is a serious tool for people who work fast on stage — efficient, direct, no hand-holding. The voice is confident and terse, the kind of gear a pro reaches for without thinking. The emotional target on stage is **calm command**: glance, read, act, no panic. Beauty serves legibility and focus, never decoration.

## Anti-references

Corda is explicitly none of these: **cluttered guitar-tab sites** (Ultimate-Guitar-style ad-heavy, cramped, amateur chord dumps); **generic SaaS dashboards** (blue-and-white card grids, hero-metric templates, startup boilerplate); **childish or gamified consumer music apps** (bright, playful, Duolingo-for-music energy); and **skeuomorphic gear** (fake wood, LED meters, imitation hardware textures). It reads as a real performance instrument, not a webapp dressed as one.

## Design Principles

- **Glanceable under pressure.** Every stage-facing view is read in a fraction of a second from a distance, one-handed, in low light. Legibility and hit-target size beat density and cleverness.
- **Zero-prep to stage.** The product does the charting work — AI-filled chords and keys, key finding — so a song goes from unknown to performable with minimal manual effort.
- **Instrument, not app.** Behave like pro gear: fast, predictable, uncluttered. No consumer gloss, no dashboard chrome, no gamification.
- **Speed over ornament.** Sharp and fast is the identity. Cut anything that slows the path from opening a song to playing it.
- **Earn the dark stage.** The dark, focused aesthetic exists because that's the real environment; design choices are validated against actual on-stage use, not a bright desktop.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG target committed. In practice, legibility is a first-class product requirement because of where Corda is used: high text contrast on the dark surface, large one-handed tap targets, glanceable-at-distance type, and reduced-motion respected in Gig Mode. Revisit a formal WCAG level (AA) if/when Corda broadens past its current single-performer usage.
